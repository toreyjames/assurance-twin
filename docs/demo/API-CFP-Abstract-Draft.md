### 3. Abstract (290 words)

Every plant exists in four states at once: as-designed, as-built, as-operated, and as-assured. The gaps between those four states are where operational and cyber risk hide. Yet most operators today have no defensible discipline for naming what they know, how they know it, with what confidence, and — most importantly — what they do not yet know but probably should.

This is an engineering-epistemology problem, and it is the problem the OT industry is finally converging on. The August 2025 CISA, NSA, EPA, FBI, and seven-country joint guidance made comprehensive asset inventory and taxonomy the foundation of a modern defensible architecture. NIST's National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence is forming a 2026 consortium project on exactly this challenge after operators named asset management their single biggest gap. SANS' 2025 survey reports asset visibility as the number-one OT investment priority, yet only 12.6 percent of organizations have full visibility across the ICS Cyber Kill Chain. SANS' 2026 work and DOE/Idaho National Laboratory formalize the discipline under Cyber-Informed Engineering.

This session presents an engineering-epistemology approach to OT asset assurance. Using a deterministic reconciliation pipeline, we ingest evidence from whichever sources an operator already has — engineering baseline, network discovery, endpoint, ITSM, OT protocol observation, historian, CMMS, vulnerability scanners, SME interviews — and reconcile their testimony into a single defensible inventory canon. Every claim carries provenance, confidence, and an epistemic state: corroborated, contradicted, or absent-but-expected. The output is decision-ready for both human assessors and the agentic actors that increasingly operate alongside them; agents specifically need explicit epistemology because, unlike humans, they cannot hold ambiguity safely.

Attendees will leave with a defensible discipline, a denominator framework that survives auditor questioning, and an honest roadmap from canon to process-aware OT defense.
